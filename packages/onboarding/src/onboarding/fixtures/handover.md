# Facilities Service Desk handover interviews

These interviews are synthetic. The roles, tickets, dates, and cases are for the demo.

## Interview 1

**November 16, 2026 · Mara Voss · Outgoing Intake Coordinator**

Recorded by Alex Neumann at the Facilities desk, 09:10–09:24. Mara retires on 30 November after eleven years on the intake rota. Names and events are synthetic.

### intake-recent-case

Last week a library colleague reported that something was wrong in the annex. I asked which room they meant and what they could see. It turned out to be a light in the service room, and they knew who would be there later if Maintenance needed access. That gave me a location, a description and a way to follow up. I searched for an existing report before opening anything. There was one from that morning, so I attached the colleague's new details to it and checked that the original ticket still had an owner. The job for our desk was done once those details were visible to the next person.

### intake-duplicate-tip

It is worth searching by building and room first when you think there may be a duplicate. The first caller might say a light has gone out and the second might say a corridor is dark. Those summaries may never match a keyword search, even if they describe the same issue. I start with open tickets for the location and compare their summaries. I still check the issue, because two unrelated faults can happen in the same room. If I find the same issue, I link the report to that ticket. When no match turns up, I log a new ticket and assign it to the Maintenance Queue.

### intake-old-routing

We used to send requests directly to the building supervisor. Someone who looks at an old printed copy might see that instruction. The shared Maintenance Queue now owns new routine work. On a busy day it is tempting to send a message to whoever answered you last time, but the queue is how the next shift sees who has the ticket. When the report is missing a building or room, ask the person and pause the intake until you can place it. We had delays caused by assigning a request with a guess about the location.

### intake-callback

I remember asking vendors to call back within four hours when I was covering the desk last spring. That was how I handled the reports from that site. I cannot point to a current guide where the four hours are written down. I would ask the Desk Lead before promising any time today.

## Interview 2

**November 20, 2026 · Selin Kaya · Outgoing Shift Coordinator**

Recorded by Alex Neumann after the operations review, 15:00–15:13. Selin transfers to another department on 27 November. The shared desk loses both experienced coordinators within the same week.

### shift-recent-case

For the shift handoff I open the dated list and check it against the tickets that are still active. I leave the next person a ticket ID and a status they can act on. If the work is already finished, I include the closure note and avoid putting it back in the pending list. When a routine request has no owner at the end of the next business day after it was logged, it needs the Desk Lead's attention. Send the ticket ID and latest status to the Desk Lead. They can see whether Maintenance already has the request and tell us who should follow up.

### shift-open-queue

Right now FM-104 is the first deadline. The Desk Lead has it and needs to confirm an access window with the library annex contact by noon on Monday, November 23. FM-103 is finished and the closure note is already attached. On Tuesday the Maintenance Queue Coordinator needs to review the community centre reports in FM-105. It is easier to keep the handoff list useful if each item has an owner, a deadline where one applies, and the next action. I would tell the incoming person to read the date on the snapshot before assuming it describes today's queue.

### shift-locations

Open the Facilities onboarding folder if you want the current Maintenance Intake Guide. The checklist gives you the questions to ask before logging anything. The handoff notes go in the Shift Handoffs folder. If you cannot find a folder, ask the Duty Intake Coordinator, who covers intake when the regular coordinator is away. If you are covering a shift, use the handoff guide for the next action and the task list for the date and owner.

### shift-callback

I was taught to expect a vendor callback by the next business day. I know some of us used a shorter time, depending on the site. The guide tells me to check with the Desk Lead, and I cannot find an approved time for routine requests. If the caller asks, I tell them I am checking the timing.

### shift-off-topic

When I first started, we used to get coffee together after the Friday shift. It helped me learn people's names. The shop changed after a few years and the practice stopped. It has no bearing on how the desk should log a request or hand over a ticket.
